CORRECTION to sample 3: I flagged Thüringerian Bratwurst as Oktoberfest

It also means the Euro-Swiss classification (smoked / cooked / fresh / dried)

"?" = I am naming the meat from what the type normally is, not your recipe.


===========================================================================
FROM LEO
===========================================================================

Bratwurst (Classic)  ?
  Coarse pork with salt, white pepper and a touch of nutmeg. Mild and meaty
  rather than spiced — the sausage every other bratwurst is measured against.

Thüringerian Bratwurst  ?
  Pork with marjoram, caraway and garlic. More strongly seasoned than a
  classic bratwurst, with marjoram leading.

English Bangers  ?
  Pork with rusk, white pepper and sage. Mild and soft-textured, more bready
  and tender than a German sausage.

Hungarian Spicy Sausage  ?
  Coarse pork with hot and sweet paprika, garlic and chilli. The hottest
  sausage on the counter, with the deep red sweetness of paprika underneath.

Spicy Italian with Fennel  ?
  Coarse pork with fennel seed and chilli. Sweet aniseed first, then a slow
  heat behind it.


===========================================================================
FROM EURO-SWISS / WERDENBERG
(their catalogue states how each one arrives — that part is fact)
===========================================================================

--- HOTDOGS ---

Beef Hotdog
  Pure beef, no pork, finely ground and mildly seasoned. Smooth and savoury
  with no coarseness to it.

Special Hotdog
  Beef with pork fat, which is what makes it richer than the pure beef one.
  Fuller and rounder in flavour, still mild.

Cheese Hotdog  ?
  Hotdog sausage with cheese worked through the mix. Mild and savoury, turning
  creamy and salty where the cheese sits.

--- SMOKED, arrive cooked through ---

Cervelat  ?
  Beef, pork and bacon, finely ground and smoked. Mild, smooth and gently
  smoky — Switzerland's everyday sausage.

Frankfurter  ?
  Finely ground pork and beef, smoked, in a slim casing. Smooth, lightly
  salty, with a clean mild smoke.

Wienerli  ?
  The slimmer Swiss wiener, smoked, sold in pairs. Mild and smooth, a little
  gentler than the frankfurter.

Cheesekrainer  ?
  Coarse smoked pork with pockets of cheese through it. Smoky and salty, with
  soft cheese running through each bite.

Hungarian Cheesy Sausage  ?
  Coarse pork heavy with sweet paprika, with cheese through it. Warm and
  smoky-sweet rather than hot, rounded off by the cheese.

Italian Garlic  ?
  Smoked pork with a lot of garlic and black pepper. Big and savoury, garlic
  first and last.

Kielbasa  ?
  Pork with garlic and marjoram, wood-smoked. Deeply savoury and smoky, firm
  rather than juicy.

--- COOKED, ready to eat ---

Breakfast Sausage Patty  ?
  Seasoned pork pressed into flat patties with sage and pepper. Savoury and
  herb-forward, softer than a sausage in a casing.

Veal Beef Bratwurst
  Veal and beef, finely ground and mildly seasoned with white pepper. Pale,
  soft and delicate — the mildest bratwurst here.

Chicken Chipolata  ?
  Lean chicken, lightly seasoned, in a thin casing. Light and delicate, the
  gentlest thing on the counter.

--- FRESH, raw ---

Mergese Sausage  ?
  Lamb with harissa, cumin, garlic and paprika. Fiery and aromatic, and
  noticeably lean next to a pork sausage.

Nuernberger Sausage  ?
  Small finger-length pork sausages seasoned with marjoram. Mild and herbal,
  with marjoram the flavour you notice first.

--- DRIED, keep without a fridge ---

Beer Sticks  ?
  Beef and pork, air-dried and lightly smoked. Dense, chewy and salty with a
  sour tang from the drying.

Buure Schueblig  ?
  Swiss farmhouse sausage of beef and pork, dried and smoked. Strongly meaty
  and smoky, with a savoury finish.

Landjaeger  ?
  Beef and pork, air-dried and cold-smoked, pressed flat. Dense and chewy,
  salty with a sour edge.
