SAMPLE 2 — taste and ingredients. Nothing written to Loyverse yet.

Format: first sentence = what is in it. Second = what it tastes like.

A "?" marks where I am describing what the TYPE normally contains, not what
YOUR product contains. Those need checking at the counter before they go live
— especially pork, see the note at the end.


Beef Hotdog
  Finely ground beef with salt, pepper and a little paprika, in a skinless
  casing. Smooth and mild, the familiar hot dog flavour with no coarseness
  to it.

Beer Sticks  ?
  Air-dried beef and pork, salted and lightly smoked. Dense and chewy, salty
  with a sour tang from the drying.

Bratwurst (Classic)  ?
  Coarse pork with salt, white pepper and a touch of nutmeg. Mild and meaty
  rather than spiced — the sausage every other bratwurst is measured against.

Breakfast Sausage Patty  ?
  Seasoned pork pressed flat, with sage and pepper. Savoury and herb-forward,
  softer than a cased sausage.

Buure Schueblig  ?
  Swiss farmhouse sausage of pork and beef, lightly smoked. Mild, faintly
  sweet, with a clean smoke that does not dominate.

Cervelat  ?
  Finely ground pork and beef, smoked. Smooth, mild and lightly smoky — the
  sausage Swiss children grow up on.

Cheese Hotdog
  Hot dog sausage with cheese worked through the mix. Mild and savoury, the
  cheese turning creamy where it melts.

Cheesekrainer  ?
  Coarse smoked pork studded with pockets of cheese. Smoky and salty, with
  bursts of soft cheese through it.

Chicken Chipolata  ?
  Lean chicken, mildly seasoned, in a thin casing. Light and delicate — the
  gentlest sausage on the counter.

English Bangers  ?
  Pork with rusk, white pepper and sage. Soft-textured and mild, more bready
  and tender than a German sausage.

Frankfurter  ?
  Finely ground pork and beef, lightly smoked. Smooth, salty and mild, with a
  clean snap to the skin.

Hungarian Cheesy Sausage  ?
  Coarse pork heavy with sweet paprika, with cheese through it. Warm and
  smoky-sweet rather than hot, softened by the cheese.

Hungarian Spicy Sausage  ?
  Coarse pork with sweet and hot paprika, garlic and chilli. The hottest
  sausage here — properly fiery, with the deep red sweetness of paprika
  underneath.

Italian Garlic  ?
  Coarse pork with garlic, salt and pepper. Big, savoury and garlic-forward.

Italian Sausage  ?
  Coarse pork with fennel seed, salt and pepper. Savoury with the sweet
  aniseed note of fennel that makes it unmistakably Italian.

Kielbasa  ?
  Pork with garlic and marjoram, smoked over wood. Deeply savoury and smoky,
  firm rather than juicy.

Landjaeger  ?
  Beef and pork, air-dried and cold-smoked. Dense, chewy and salty, with a
  sour tang from the drying.

Mergese Sausage  ?
  Lamb with harissa, cumin, garlic and paprika. Fiery and aromatic, and
  noticeably lean next to a pork sausage.

Nuernberger Sausage  ?
  Finger-sized pork sausages seasoned with marjoram. Mild and herbal, with
  marjoram as the flavour you notice first.

Special Hotdog  ??
  I do not know what makes this one special — tell me and I will write it.

Spicy Italian with Fennel  ?
  Coarse pork with fennel seed and chilli. Sweet aniseed first, then a slow
  heat behind it.

Thüringerian Bratwurst  ?
  Pork with marjoram, caraway and garlic. More strongly seasoned than a
  classic bratwurst, with marjoram leading.

Veal Beef Bratwurst  ?
  Veal and beef, finely ground, mildly seasoned. Pale, soft and delicate —
  milder than any pork bratwurst.

White Sausage
  Veal and pork, very finely ground, with parsley, lemon zest and mace.
  Delicate, milky and barely seasoned — the mildest thing on the counter.

Wienerli  ?
  Finely ground pork and beef, lightly smoked, in a slim casing. Smooth and
  mild with a gentle smoke.

BEFORE ANY OF THIS GOES LIVE

Nearly every line above names pork. That is what these sausage types normally
contain — but I have not seen your recipes, and in the Philippines whether a
product contains pork is not a detail. A Muslim customer reading "beef" and
getting pork is a real harm, not a typo.

So: someone at the counter needs to confirm the meat in each one before these
are published. Same for the three that could carry allergens — the rusk in
the bangers is wheat, and the cheese ones are dairy.

If that check is too much work right now, the safe version is to drop the
ingredient sentence and keep only the taste. Say the word and I will rewrite
them that way.
