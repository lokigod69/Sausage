Beef Hotdog
  Smooth, finely ground beef sausage in a skinless casing — the familiar hot dog, sold by weight rather than in a sealed pack. Simmer in water just off the boil for five minutes; a rolling boil splits them.

Beer Sticks
  Dried, firm snacking sausage, cut from the length rather than cooked. Keeps for weeks in the fridge and needs nothing done to it.

Bratwurst (Classic)
  The everyday German bratwurst: coarse pork in a natural casing, pale and plump, raw. Fry on a medium heat for twelve to fifteen minutes and turn often — too hot and the skin splits before the middle is done.

Breakfast Sausage Patty
  Seasoned sausage meat pressed into flat patties, no casing, ready for the pan. Four to five minutes a side in a dry pan is enough; they release their own fat.

Buure Schueblig
  Swiss farmhouse sausage, lightly smoked and already cooked through. Heat it gently in water at about 80 °C for ten minutes — boiling bursts the skin and the flavour goes into the pot.

Cervelat
  Switzerland's everyday sausage: short, thick, smooth and smoked, eaten hot or cold. Score the ends in a cross before grilling and they curl open.

Cheese Hotdog
  Hot dog sausage with cheese worked through it, so it melts as it heats. Grill or pan-fry rather than boil, or the cheese leaches out.

Cheesekrainer
  Austrian smoked sausage with pockets of cheese inside, sold by the kilo. Grill it until the skin blisters — the cheese should be running when you cut it.

Chicken Chipolata
  Thin chicken sausages, mild and lean, sold in their links. They cook faster than a pork sausage and dry out quickly, so eight minutes on a medium heat is plenty.

English Bangers
  The British breakfast sausage: pork, pale and soft-textured, with rusk in the mix that makes it tender rather than springy. Cook slowly — the name comes from what happens when the pan is too hot.

Frankfurter
  Long, slender, lightly smoked and already cooked. Hot water for five minutes and no further; frankfurters are ruined by boiling.

Hungarian Cheesy Sausage
  Paprika-red Hungarian sausage with cheese through it. Grill or pan-fry on a medium heat so the cheese softens without escaping.

Hungarian Spicy Sausage
  Coarse pork heavy with paprika and chilli — the hottest sausage on this counter. Good in a stew or with potatoes, where the fat carries the spice through the dish.

Italian Garlic
  Coarse Italian pork sausage with garlic through it, raw. Squeeze it out of the casing for a ragù, or leave it whole and roast it with peppers.

Italian Sausage
  Coarse pork with fennel seed, the classic Italian butcher's sausage. The meat out of the casing is the base of a proper ragù; whole, it wants a slow pan.

Kielbasa
  Thick Polish smoked sausage, already cooked, sold in its horseshoe. Slice it into a pan of cabbage or potatoes — it is a seasoning as much as a meat.

Landjaeger
  Flat, dark, air-dried snacking sausage that keeps without a fridge. Eat it as it is, with bread and a beer; cooking it is a waste.

Mergese Sausage
  Merguez: thin North African lamb sausage, deep red with harissa and cumin. Very lean, so it cooks in minutes and turns dry if you leave it on.

Nuernberger Sausage
  Small finger-length pork sausages from Nuremberg, seasoned with marjoram. Traditionally eaten three or six at a time; two minutes a side on a hot pan.

Special Hotdog
  A plumper hot dog than the standard beef one, sold by weight. Simmer gently or grill — never a hard boil.

Spicy Italian with Fennel
  Coarse Italian pork with fennel seed and chilli. The heat is in the sausage rather than the sauce, so it suits a plain tomato base.

Thüringerian Bratwurst
  Long, slim bratwurst from Thuringia, seasoned with marjoram and caraway. Grilled over charcoal is the point of it; a pan works but loses the smoke.

Veal Beef Bratwurst
  Pale, finely textured bratwurst of veal and beef — milder and softer than the pork classic. Gentle heat only; it has little fat to protect it.

White Sausage
  Bavarian Weisswurst: very pale, delicate, veal and pork with parsley and lemon. Heat in water at 70-80 °C for ten minutes and never boil it, then eat the inside and leave the skin.

Wienerli
  Slim Swiss wiener, smoked and already cooked, sold in pairs. Hot water for a few minutes is all it needs.
